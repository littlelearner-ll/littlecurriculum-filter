Out-of-Scope and Beyond-K12 Filter Rule 
Some inputs cannot be reliably classified into K5, K8, or K12 because they are ill-formed or exceed secondary-school scope. In such cases, output OOS. This includes:1) Ill-formed or underspecified texts, where comprehension fails due to fragmentary structure, missing context or referents, unexplained symbolic jumps, or dense technical jargon without scaffolding.2) Post-K12 content, requiring knowledge beyond high school, such as calculus, abstract algebra, real analysis/topology/measure theory, advanced theoretical linear algebra, university-level proof systems, or specialized research mathematics. A text must not be classified as K12 merely because it is difficult or unclear; K12 requires identifiable secondary-level structures rather than obscurity or post-secondary abstraction.


Hard Cutoff Principle
If a text exhibits any feature that requires comprehension strategies, background knowledge, or reasoning processes characteristic of a higher grade band, the text is classified at that higher band, even if it also satisfies all requirements of lower bands.
Age-Appropriate Representation Constraint
A text is appropriate for a grade band only if the concepts it presents are expressed using representational systems (language, symbols, formalisms, abstractions) that are typically accessible to readers at that developmental stage. Representational systems include not only linguistic forms but also symbolic, mathematical, computational, or diagrammatic conventions.
Lower-Band Constraint Preservation
Texts classified at a higher grade band must still meet the minimum clarity, coherence, and explicitness requirements of lower grade bands; texts that rely on obscurity, omission, or unexplained jumps in reasoning are not upgraded but considered ill-formed or out of scope.
